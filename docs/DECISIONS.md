# Decisions

Every place where the brief could be read more than one way, and the reading this app implements.
Each decision is enforced by a test in `backend/trips/tests` unless marked otherwise.

| # | Ambiguity in the brief | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | "Sample every 10 / 25 / 50 miles" | The user picks 10, 25 or 50 (default 25). Checkpoints sit at mile 0, N, 2N, … and at the destination; a regular point closer than N/4 to the destination is dropped. | Exactly the three values the brief names. No near-duplicate final sample. |
| D2 | Band boundaries (is 0.25 in/hr Moderate or High?) | Every band is lower-bound inclusive. For rain and snow the top of Severe is inclusive (1.00 and 3.0 are Severe) because No Travel is defined as strictly greater. | The only reading where the table has no gaps or overlaps. |
| D3 | Integer wind bands (25–34, 35–44) vs decimal forecasts | Treated as continuous half-open intervals: 34.6 mph is Moderate, 35.0 is High. Values are rounded half-up first (wind 1 dp, precipitation 2 dp). | Forecasts are decimals; rounding stops float noise (0.249999) from changing a band. |
| D4 | Sustained wind or gusts? | Classify on sustained 10 m wind only. Gusts are returned for reference but never classified, so a missing gust value never makes a checkpoint Unknown. | The brief says "wind". |
| D5 | Weather at the ETA, from hourly data | Wind is linearly interpolated between the hourly stamps either side of the ETA. Rain, showers and snowfall are hourly *totals*, taken from the hour the truck is in (the hour starting at floor(ETA)); totals are never interpolated. Rain = `rain + showers`; `precipitation` is not used because it includes snow water equivalent. | Matches Open-Meteo's variable semantics (instantaneous vs preceding-hour sums). |
| D6 | Combining wind, rain and snow | Checkpoint risk = worst of the three. The "driver" sentence names the condition that set it (ties: wind, then rain, then snow). | Worst-of is the only safe reading. |
| D7 | Scope of the load rules | Wind only, strict `>` on weight (exactly 30,000 / 40,000 lb do not trigger), and they can only raise a level. | The brief's wording. |
| D8 | What "load weight" is | The weight the rules compare, in lb, 1 to 80,000 (the US federal gross limit). It is also sent to the routing engine as truck weight (metric tons). | Thresholds are in lb. |
| D9 | Miles at each level | Each checkpoint owns the road half-way to its neighbours (midpoint ownership). Miles per level therefore sum exactly to the route length. | No double counting, no gaps. |
| D10 | "Average risk" | Distance-weighted: each checkpoint's ordinal score (Low 0 … No Travel 4) weighted by the miles it covers, so a short end stretch counts less than a full interval. Miles with no forecast count as High (2). | "Average risk along the route" is a per-mile quantity; a plain mean over checkpoints over-weights the short first and last stretches. |
| D11 | Ranking, and where No Travel fits | The brief's order, literally: (Severe-or-worse miles, High miles, average risk, duration). No-Travel miles count inside "Severe miles" (No Travel is worse than Severe); miles with no forecast count inside "High miles" (D13). Miles compare at 0.1 mi and average risk at 2 dp, the precision the UI shows, so a sentence never contradicts the ranking. No other keys. | The brief defines the balance between risk and time; it is applied as written. Counting No Travel inside Severe keeps a No-Travel route from looking clean. |
| D12 | Slider time base | Hour 0 = the chosen departure time; the slider covers hours 0 to 48 inclusive (49 values). | Shows the weather the truck will actually drive into. |
| D13 | Missing forecasts | A checkpoint with no forecast is **Unknown**, never Low. It is drawn dashed, counted in `miles.unknown`, and counted as High (score 2, High miles) for the average and the ranking, so a gap in the data can never make a route look safer. A warning names the affected checkpoints. If every weather request fails the API returns 502 instead of a result. | Missing data must never look safe, or win. |
| D14 | Time zones | The departure is entered in the **origin's** local time (the zone comes with the geocoded place). The server works in UTC. Every ETA is shown in the local time of the place it describes. | Cross-country trips cross zones, and dispatchers plan from elsewhere. |
| D15 | Fewer than three routes | Ask for two alternates; when a Stadia key is set and Stadia returns fewer than three routes, also ask the public server and merge. Drop any route that is effectively the same road as an earlier one (length within 2% and midpoints within 2 mi). If fewer than three remain, ask the same routing service again for a route **through one waypoint** to the side of the direct line (12% of the straight-line trip from its midpoint, then 25%, both sides; at most four extra requests, in parallel). The service still plans every road. A detour is kept only if it is a different road, at most 50% slower than the fastest route, and does not drive out to the waypoint and back (no stretch re-running road it already used); it carries a notice saying it was added as a detour. Failed detour requests are skipped. If fewer than three still remain, show them with a notice. | The brief asks for three options. Valhalla returns no alternates where one interstate dominates (Dallas to Houston, New York to Los Angeles); slower state-highway corridors are real options a dispatcher weighs when the interstate has weather, and the ranking still decides on risk first. |
| D16 | Driver rest | Continuous driving is assumed; hours-of-service breaks are out of scope. | Scope control; stated in the README. |
| D17 | Explanation | One sentence names the first ranking criterion that separated the winner, and only the routes that actually tied on the earlier criteria. A second sentence gives the time cost against the fastest route and why that route lost. The verdict's route lanes print the criteria for every route (Severe-or-worse miles, High-or-unknown miles, average risk) with the deciding one marked. | A reader can check the recommendation against the figures beside it. |
| D18 | Departure window | Departure must be within [now − 1 h, now + 14 days]. ETAs past the forecast horizon become Unknown. | The forecast covers 16 days. |
| D19 | Routing provider | **Valhalla** (`costing=truck`, public FOSSGIS instance) instead of HERE. One request returns the route plus two alternates with truck restrictions and the truck's weight applied. Time along the route comes from Valhalla's per-maneuver times, spread over each maneuver's shape by distance. | Keyless: a reviewer can clone and run without signing up. The provider sits behind a small `RoutingProvider` protocol, so HERE or a self-hosted Valhalla (`VALHALLA_URL`) is a drop-in. With `STADIA_API_KEY` set, Stadia Maps' hosted Valhalla (same API) is primary and the public server is the fallback, used only on an outage; "no route" is never retried elsewhere. |
| D20 | Geocoding provider | **Photon** (OpenStreetMap, keyless). Typeahead never fails loudly: a provider error returns no suggestions. | Same reason as D19. |
| D21 | Corridor heat rendering | A blurred *categorical* circle layer coloured by the exact level at each corridor point, not a density heatmap. Low is left bare so hazards read first. The slider track carries the 48 hours at a glance (the worst level anywhere in the corridor each hour), and a readout names the level, its share of the corridor and where it starts on the selected route. | A density heatmap glows wherever points cluster, regardless of risk. The track and readout make a calm day visibly calm instead of looking empty. |
| D22 | When even the best route crosses No Travel | The verdict says **Do not depart** as its headline; the recommended route is shown as the least exposed one. The app estimates the earliest whole-hour delay (1–48 h) after which that route meets no No-Travel conditions, reading each checkpoint at its nearest corridor point at the hour the truck would reach it after the delay; missing data never counts as clear. It offers a button that re-runs the full analysis at that departure, so the estimate is always confirmed by the real pipeline. | The brief's ranking always names a route; when that route is still unsafe the honest instruction is to wait, and the 48 h corridor forecast already holds enough to say how long. |
| D23 | Weather rate limits | Corridor heat points are capped across all routes together (150), so extra routes widen the heat spacing instead of multiplying weather requests. If the weather service refuses any request for rate limiting (HTTP 429 after retries), the analysis returns 503 `weather_busy` ("try again in a minute") instead of scoring those places as Unknown. | Throttled places are not "no forecast": counting them as High would skew the ranking. The free Open-Meteo tier limits locations per minute. |
